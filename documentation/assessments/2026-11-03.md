## Rotationsgeschwindigkeit und Phase-Wind-up: Fragen und Antworten

### Wie groß ist die Rotationsgeschwindigkeit des Drehtisches?

**Antwort:** Aus der mittleren Umdrehungsdauer folgt `omega = 360° / T`. Die aus den vollständigen Umdrehungen berechneten Werte sind:

:::equation rotation

| Datensatz | T [s] | omega [°/s] |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2026-11-03 | 169,599 | 2,123 |
| 2026-11-07 | 181,215 | 1,987 |

Dies ist die aus der mittleren Periodendauer abgeleitete effektive Rotationsrate. Die momentane Winkelgeschwindigkeit schwankt. Es ist weder der Mittelwert einzelner `360°/T_j` noch eine Aussage über die Rotation innerhalb ausgeschlossener Referenzlücken.

### Ist bei dieser Rotationsrate Phase-Wind-up zu berücksichtigen?

**Antwort:** Bei idealer Rotation um die aufrechte Antennenachse kann der gemeinsame Empfängeranteil innerhalb derselben Trägerfrequenz durch die RTK-Satellitendifferenzbildung entfallen. Roll/Pitch erzeugen dagegen geometrieabhängige Anteile. Die reine Yaw-Annahme ist eine Bedingung für diese Einordnung; aus GGA ist die tatsächliche Antennenlage nicht nachgewiesen. [R12](https://www.ion.org/publications/abstract.cfm?articleID=9705), [R13](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-66794-6)

Die Größenordnung lässt sich für den 07.11.2026 berechnen: Eine ideale volle axiale Drehung entspricht einer Trägerperiode. Mit der GPS-L1-Wellenlänge von 190,294 mm ergibt sich aus 181,215 s pro Umdrehung ein Betrag von **1,050 mm/s**; nach 300 s sind es etwa **315 mm Trägerphasenänderung**. Für den 03.11.2026 ergibt die entsprechende Rechnung **1,122 mm/s**. Wind-up betrifft die Trägerphase; die Phasenentwicklung muss stetig über ganze Zyklen fortgeführt werden. [R11](https://gssc.esa.int/navipedia/index.php/Carrier_Phase_Wind-up_Effect)

:::equation windup

### Was bedeutet dies für den Empfängervergleich und die Outages?

**Antwort:** Die genannten mm/s beschreiben einen entfernungsäquivalenten Phasenanteil. Seine Abbildung auf die Position hängt vom Beobachtungsmodell ab; er ist keine HRMS-/VRMS-Fitsteigung. Eine langsame Drehung allein rechtfertigt deshalb keine Vernachlässigung.

Für den reinen gemeinsamen Rover-Yaw-Anteil folgt aus demselben Doppeldifferenzmodell auch während eines RTCM-Ausfalls die Auslöschung zwischen Satelliten. Dies ist eine Modellfolgerung, kein Nachweis der implementierten Outage-Verfahren. Undifferenzierte Fortführung, Mehrfrequenz-Biaszustände und Antennenverkippung erfordern eine eigene Prüfung. Satellitenseitiger Wind-up bleibt ein gesonderter Modellterm.

Die gemeinsame Antenne führt zunächst denselben physikalischen Anteil in die Receiver-Eingänge ein. Ihre Positionsausgabe kann ihn je nach Verarbeitung unterschiedlich abbilden; die kontinuierliche FMI-Referenz ist daher kein unabhängiger Wind-up-Nachweis. Unsere GGA-Auswertung erlaubt keine Beurteilung dieser internen Modelle. Eine Korrektur gehört gegebenenfalls in die Rohphasenverarbeitung; ein pauschales Abziehen von 1,050 mm/s von ausgegebenen Positionen ist nicht begründet. Zur Prüfung eignen sich Rohphasen und Lageinformationen sowie ein Vergleichsversuch mit festgehaltener Antennenausrichtung.

## Method and time alignment

NMEA checksums are verified and invalid positions (quality 0) are discarded. Literal `\r\n` sequences are supported. Time alignment uses the UTC epochs of the GGA messages, rather than file start times or line numbers. Midnight rollovers are detected. The date, 3 November 2026, comes from UM980 RMC messages; the same date is assumed for both P23A files because they contain no date information. An unknown internal measurement latency cannot be independently verified using GGA alone; no subsequent time shift is estimated.

WGS84 latitude/longitude are converted to local east/north coordinates in metres using local WGS84 radii of curvature, which is sufficient for this small measurement area. A geometric circle fit with Huber weights determines the centre and radius from all available RTK-fixed P23A_0 positions (GGA quality 4).

The reference angle is `atan2(north−centre_north, east−centre_east)`, positive from east towards north. The rotation plot shows the relative angle divided by 360° as the number of revolutions since the first reference point, including fractional turns. The CSV files also include this revolution count. The angle is unwrapped over time; a constant rotation rate is not assumed. Radial reference outliers exceeding max(2 cm, 6 robust standard deviations) are excluded from angle determination. No fixed reference points were excluded by this criterion in these data.

Analysis uses the common time interval with available fixed reference data. Where necessary, the unwrapped angle is interpolated between adjacent reliable reference epochs, across no more than `--max-gap` seconds. There is no extrapolation. Before the RTK-fixed filter, all three receivers have exactly matching timestamps for the 13,191 time-aligned epochs, so interpolation is unnecessary for these data. After filtering, each receiver retains only its own RTK-fixed epochs; alignment to the reference remains unchanged. Two larger reference gaps are excluded. The number of complete revolutions during these gaps is unknown; the angle resumes at the nearest equivalent angle. Segment numbers are recorded in `reference_angle.csv`.

## Rotation rate and phase wind-up: questions and answers

### What is the turn-table rotation rate?

**Answer:** The mean revolution period gives `omega = 360° / T`. The values derived from complete revolutions are:

:::equation rotation

| Dataset | T [s] | omega [°/s] |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2026-11-03 | 169.599 | 2.123 |
| 2026-11-07 | 181.215 | 1.987 |

This is the effective rotation rate derived from the mean period. Instantaneous angular velocity varies. It is neither the mean of individual `360°/T_j` values nor a measurement of rotation during excluded reference gaps.

### Must phase wind-up be considered at this rotation rate?

**Answer:** Under ideal rotation about the upright antenna axis, the common receiver contribution can disappear through RTK between-satellite differencing within one carrier frequency. Roll/pitch instead introduce geometry-dependent terms. Pure yaw is a condition for this interpretation; actual antenna attitude has not been established from GGA. [R12](https://www.ion.org/publications/abstract.cfm?articleID=9705), [R13](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-66794-6)

For the November 7 example, an ideal complete axial turn corresponds to one carrier cycle. GPS L1 has a wavelength of 190.294 mm, giving a magnitude of **1.050 mm/s** for a 181.215 s revolution, or approximately **315 mm of carrier-phase change** after 300 s. The equivalent November 3 calculation gives **1.122 mm/s**. Wind-up acts on carrier phase; its evolution must remain continuous across full cycles. [R11](https://gssc.esa.int/navipedia/index.php/Carrier_Phase_Wind-up_Effect)

:::equation windup

### What follows for the receiver comparison and outages?

**Answer:** These mm/s describe a range-equivalent phase term. Its mapping into position depends on the observation model; it is not an HRMS/VRMS fitted slope. Slow rotation alone therefore does not justify neglecting it.

Within the same double-difference model, the purely common rover-yaw contribution also cancels between satellites during an RTCM outage. This is a model deduction, not verification of the implemented outage algorithms. Undifferenced propagation, multi-frequency bias states and antenna tilt require separate checking. Satellite-side wind-up remains a distinct model term.

The shared antenna initially introduces the same physical contribution at the receiver inputs. Different processing can map it differently into position, so the continuously corrected FMI reference is not an independent wind-up validation. Our GGA analysis cannot establish those internal models. Any required correction belongs in raw carrier-phase processing; subtracting 1.050 mm/s from output positions is unjustified. Raw phase and attitude records, plus a comparison run with antenna orientation held fixed, can support a targeted check.

## Assessment against the earlier study (steps 1–6)

**The current receivers achieve about 3–4 cm HRMS with corrections approximately five minutes old. This is substantially below earlier steps 1–5, but above the result obtained with precise satellite clocks in step 6.** Position outputs alone do not identify the methods implemented inside today's receivers.

Source: [R1], steps 1–6 on pages 1–2 and Figure 1 on page 2. Historical values at 300 s below are approximate reconstructions from plotted curves; step 6 is a single point described as about 20 mm in the text. Our 34.2 mm (P23A_1) and 36.2 mm (UM980) come from 290 < dAge ≤300 s, not fit predictions. P23A_1 ends at 299 s.

**1. Use outdated reference data unchanged:** Earlier errors grew almost linearly to about 829 mm HRMS at 300 s. Our 34–36 mm show much more favourable behaviour. Correction age alone does not determine accuracy; processing methods and test conditions matter.

**2. Apply broadcast satellite clock models:** Errors fell to about 687 mm. Changes in satellite clocks matter, but the simple clock model was insufficient in the earlier experiment.

**3. Add the relativistic correction:** Errors fell further to about 347 mm. Model terms that largely cancel for simultaneous observations become relevant when reference and rover epochs differ. Careful modelling is essential.

**4. Use ultra-rapid orbits and clocks:** Errors fell to about 134 mm. Better satellite information substantially reduced errors. Today's results are lower still; different experimental conditions prevent an isolated algorithm comparison.

**5. Use final precise orbits:** The additional improvement to about 105 mm was limited. Orbit errors were no longer the main bottleneck in that experiment. More accurate orbits alone did not solve the problem.

**6. Use final precise satellite clocks:** About 20 mm at 300 s. The earlier report attributes the remaining error to short-term satellite clock nonlinearities. This is a plausible hypothesis to test for our residuals, not an established cause. The historical point uses precise products available after the experiment and does not establish a real-time accuracy limit.

**What do we learn?** Current results are very good, but do not establish that an accuracy limit has been reached. Linear error growth does not uniquely identify its cause. Our slopes of 0.0927 mm/s (P23A_1) and 0.1026 mm/s (UM980) are empirical fits, not physical limits.

P23A_1's advantage narrows during long outages: with fresh corrections (0 < dAge ≤10 s), HRMS is 4.2 versus 9.8 mm; near 300 s it is 34.2 versus 36.2 mm. These age classes use each receiver's available fixed epochs separately and are not a paired comparison.

**Next verification:** Process the same outage raw observations with different satellite clock and orbit models, use independent position truth, and assess fixed-solution availability. Our current analysis evaluates retained fixed solutions relative to the P23A_0 expected circle. Common errors can remain hidden. It demonstrates good relative outage accuracy, not an absolute accuracy limit.

## 旋转速率与相位缠绕：问题与回答

### 转台的旋转速率是多少？

**回答：** 由平均旋转周期可得 `omega = 360° / T`。根据完整旋转周期计算的结果为：

:::equation rotation

| 数据集 | T [s] | omega [°/s] |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2026-11-03 | 169.599 | 2.123 |
| 2026-11-07 | 181.215 | 1.987 |

这是由平均周期得到的有效旋转速率，瞬时角速度会变化。它既不是各次 `360°/T_j` 的算术平均值，也不代表被排除的参考数据缺口期间的旋转测量。

### 在此旋转速率下需要考虑相位缠绕吗？

**回答：** 在绕直立天线轴进行理想旋转时，同一载频内的接收机公共项可通过 RTK 星间差分消除。横滚和俯仰则产生与几何有关的项。纯偏航是这一判断的条件，GGA 数据并未证明天线的实际姿态。[R12](https://www.ion.org/publications/abstract.cfm?articleID=9705)、[R13](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-66794-6)

以11月7日为例，一次理想的轴向完整旋转对应一个载波周期。GPS L1 波长为190.294 mm，旋转周期181.215秒对应的变化率绝对值为 **1.050 mm/s**，300秒后约为 **315 mm的载波相位变化**。11月3日的相应计算为 **1.122 mm/s**。相位缠绕作用于载波相位，其变化应跨越整周保持连续。[R11](https://gssc.esa.int/navipedia/index.php/Carrier_Phase_Wind-up_Effect)

:::equation windup

### 这对接收机对比和中断处理意味着什么？

**回答：** 上述 mm/s 表示等效距离形式的相位项。它如何映射到位置取决于观测模型，并非 HRMS/VRMS 拟合斜率。因此，不能仅因旋转缓慢就忽略该项。

在相同双差模型中，纯公共的流动站偏航项在 RTCM 中断期间也会在卫星之间抵消。这是模型推论，并非对实际中断算法的验证。非差分传播、多频偏差状态和天线倾斜需要单独检查；卫星端相位缠绕仍是另一个模型项。

共用天线首先在各接收机输入端引入相同的物理项。不同处理可能将其映射为不同的位置表现，因此持续接收改正的 FMI 参考接收机并非独立的相位缠绕验证。仅凭本次 GGA 分析无法确定内部模型。必要的改正应施加于原始载波相位处理，不能将1.050 mm/s直接从输出位置中扣除。原始相位、姿态记录以及保持天线朝向固定的对照试验可用于针对性验证。

## 对照历史研究的评价（步骤 1–6）

**当前接收机在改正数据龄期约五分钟时达到约 3–4 cm HRMS，明显低于历史步骤 1–5 的结果，但尚未达到步骤 6 使用精密卫星钟差时的结果。** 仅凭位置输出，无法确定当前接收机内部采用了哪些算法。

来源：[R1]第 1–2 页步骤 1–6 及第 2 页图 1。以下 300 秒历史值为图中曲线重建的近似值；步骤 6 是单独一点，原文约为 20 mm。当前 P23A_1 的 34.2 mm 和 UM980 的 36.2 mm 来自 290 < dAge ≤300 秒区间，并非拟合预测值。P23A_1 的数据最多到 299 秒。

**1. 直接使用过时的参考数据：** 历史误差近似线性增长，300 秒时约为 829 mm HRMS。当前 34–36 mm 的表现明显更好。改正龄期本身并不决定精度，处理方法和测试条件同样重要。

**2. 应用广播卫星钟差模型：** 历史误差降至约 687 mm。卫星钟差随时间的变化很重要，但简单钟差模型在历史实验中仍不足以解决问题。

**3. 加入相对论修正：** 历史误差进一步降至约 347 mm。参考站和流动站观测不同步时，原本在同步观测中大幅抵消的模型项会变得重要，因此精细建模十分关键。

**4. 使用超快速轨道和钟差：** 历史误差降至约 134 mm。更好的卫星信息明显降低了误差。当前结果更低，但由于实验条件不同，不能据此单独比较算法优劣。

**5. 使用最终精密轨道：** 历史误差仅进一步降至约 105 mm。在该实验中，轨道误差已不再是主要瓶颈；仅提高轨道精度并未解决问题。

**6. 使用最终精密卫星钟差：** 300 秒时约 20 mm。历史报告将剩余误差归因于卫星钟差的短期非线性变化。对当前残差而言，这是合理的待检验假设，尚非已证实原因。历史点使用事后可用的精密产品，不能视为已证实的实时精度极限。

**我们学到了什么？** 当前结果很好，但尚不能证明已经达到精度极限。线性误差增长不能唯一确定误差来源。当前 P23A_1 的 0.0927 mm/s 和 UM980 的 0.1026 mm/s 是经验拟合斜率，并非物理极限。

P23A_1 在长时间中断时的优势缩小：新鲜改正数据（0 < dAge ≤10 秒）下 HRMS 为 4.2 mm 对 9.8 mm；接近 300 秒时为 34.2 mm 对 36.2 mm。这些龄期区间分别采用各接收机可用的固定解历元，并非配对比较。

**下一步验证：** 对同一组中断原始观测应用不同卫星钟差和轨道模型，采用独立位置真值，并同时评价固定解可用性。当前分析仅评价相对于 P23A_0 理论圆周的保留固定解，共同误差可能被掩盖。因此，它证明了良好的相对中断精度，尚不能证明绝对精度极限。
